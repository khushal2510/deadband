Control valves and actuators

The final element does the work. Most loop problems that look like tuning problems are valve problems: wrong size, stiction, wrong characteristic or a slow actuator.

Actuators

  • Pneumatic spring-diaphragm: the standard. The spring sets the fail position.
  • Pneumatic piston: higher thrust or torque. Spring return or a volume tank can provide the fail action.
  • Electric: no air supply needed. Fail-safe needs a spring return or battery, otherwise it fails in place.
  • Hydraulic: very high force and speed, with an accumulator for fail-safe action.

Positioners and accessories

  • A positioner compares the signal with the stem position and drives the actuator air until they match, overcoming friction.
  • Digital positioners give diagnostics such as friction, travel and partial stroke test results.
  • Boosters and large tubing give fast stroke times for anti-surge and ESD valves.
  • Solenoid valves let the safety system override the control signal.
  • Limit switches or position transmitters give independent open and closed feedback.

Why alarms and controllers need a deadband

The signal below hovers near the alarm limit. Without a deadband the alarm sets and clears over and over. A deadband makes it clear only once the signal has moved well away from the limit.

PID in one minute

  • P reacts to the present error. More gain is a faster response and less stability.
  • I removes the steady offset by accumulating error. Too much gives overshoot and oscillation.
  • D reacts to the rate of change. Useful on slow processes, but amplifies noise, so filter it.
  • Flow and pressure loops are fast and usually PI. Temperature and level can be slow.
  • Use anti-windup and output limits, and bumpless transfer between manual and auto.
