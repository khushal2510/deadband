Why this matters to a controls engineer

Delay between action and response is dead time. Loops with large dead time must be tuned slowly because the controller cannot see the effect of its last move. A Mars rover is the extreme case, so it is told what to do in batches and decides small things on its own.

Instrumentation, but farther away

Spacecraft are control systems with the hardest possible operating conditions. A few parallels with the plant you work on.

Dead time

A command to Mars arrives minutes later and the answer takes just as long to come back. Operators plan sequences and verify later, rather than closing a loop by hand.

Thermal control

The James Webb Space Telescope keeps its instruments at roughly 40 K (about −233 °C) using a five-layer sunshield, a passive temperature control design with no process fluid.

Redundancy and voting

Critical spacecraft functions have redundant sensors and computers and cross-check them, the same idea as 2oo3 voting on a plant trip.

Safe mode is a trip

When a spacecraft detects a fault it enters safe mode: it points at the Sun, switches to a minimum configuration and waits for instructions. That is a shutdown to a known safe state.

Long service life

Voyager 1 and 2, launched in 1977, are still sending data from beyond the heliosphere. Designing for decades with no maintenance visit is the ultimate proof test interval.

Power from heat

The Voyagers run on radioisotope thermoelectric generators, which turn the heat of decaying plutonium-238 into electricity using thermocouples. Seebeck's 1821 effect, still working in deep space.
